How the Human Brain Approaches Sudoku

How human intuition maps directly to this solver's logic across any board size

People rarely guess blindly cell by cell — we scan for forced numbers, keep tiny pencil marks in tight squares, and only test a hypothesis when pure deduction pauses.
1
Cross-Out Elimination (Candidate Bitmasks)
Human mind: Look along a cell's row, column, and box to cross out digits that are already used.
Solver mirror: candidateMask() merges row, column, and box masks in one bitwise check for any N×N grid.
2
Spot the Lone Survivor (Naked Single)
Human mind: "Every other number is blocked here, so this empty square has only one legal digit left."
Solver mirror: Immediately fills any cell with 1 candidate bit, then ripples updates only to affected peers.
3
Hunt for a Digit's Only Home (Hidden Single)
Human mind: Scan an entire row, column, or box and ask: "Where can a missing digit like 7 still fit?"
Solver mirror: checkHiddenSingles() finds digits that appear as a candidate in just one cell of a unit.
4
Pencil Marks & Best-Odds Guessing (MRV)
Human mind: When logic stalls, jot pencil marks and pick a 50/50 square with just 2 options instead of 7.
Solver mirror: The Minimum Remaining Values (MRV) heuristic always branches on the tightest empty cell first.
5
Spot Dead Ends & Rewind (Contradiction & Backtrack)
Human mind: Notice a clash ("two 5s forced in one box!") and erase steps back to your last assumption.
Solver mirror: Dual contradiction detection halts immediately and the LIFO undo log restores the board cleanly.
